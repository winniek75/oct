Gotokuji: Visit Tokyo's Lucky Cat Temple by Bike

2026-08-29 · 4 min read · 日本語の要約は記事末尾にあります

Ask most visitors where the maneki-neko — Japan's famous "beckoning cat" — comes from, and they won't know the answer is a quiet temple in Setagaya, just 15 minutes by bicycle from our shop.

Gotokuji Temple is where the legend began: a poor monk's cat is said to have beckoned a feudal lord out of a thunderstorm and into the temple, bringing the monk good fortune. Today, thousands of white cat figurines — from thumb-sized to knee-high — fill the temple grounds, left by visitors whose wishes came true.

Why go by bicycle?

Gotokuji sits on the tiny two-car Setagaya Line, which most tourists never find. By bike, it becomes the highlight of an easy loop: quiet residential lanes, the retro shopping street at Shoin-jinja-mae, and Boro-ichi Street, home of a 400-year-old flea market. No transfers, no crowds — just the Tokyo that locals actually live in.

Ride tip: Go in the morning. The soft light on the rows of white cats makes the best photos, and the grounds are nearly empty before 10 a.m. Temple grounds are open roughly 6:00–17:00 and admission is free.

The route (about 8 km round trip)

From our shop, head north along the Setagaya Line — the little green train will keep you company. Stop at Shoin Shrine, then continue to Gotokuji. On the way back, detour through Boro-ichi Street for coffee. A city bike is perfectly fine for this route; the hills are gentle.

What to bring home

The temple office sells maneki-neko figurines in several sizes. Buy one, make a wish, and — if it comes true — tradition says you bring the cat back and leave it at the temple. Consider it a reason to return to Tokyo.
